What is in it

The profile underlying the Luo–Hou scenario for 3D axisymmetric Euler with boundary is solved as a root problem with an explicit sparse Jacobian, rather than by marching or by neural approximation — a third route with a different failure mode from either existing computation.

  • An exact one-parameter scaling symmetry under which α is invariant, verified symbolically and confirmed on the converged root to 2.8×10−12.
  • Linear stability certified without trusting any eigenvalue, by a Lyapunov certificate whose verification reduces to an absolute inequality — necessary here, because the rightmost eigenvalue is shown to be unconverged as a discretization.
  • A false root: a state converging to 5.4×10−14, distinct under deflation, stable under wedge truncation, morphologically coherent, and nevertheless an artifact.

The part worth borrowing

Residual, distinctness, parameter stability and morphological coherence are jointly insufficient to certify a self-similar profile. The gauge closure leaves one functional unused — the corner identity h_id = c_l − 2Θxx/Wx — and reporting it beside every solve separates the true profile from the artifact by three orders of magnitude, at a cost of one extra line of output.
